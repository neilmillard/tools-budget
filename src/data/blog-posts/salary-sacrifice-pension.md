# Salary Sacrifice Pensions: The National Insurance Saving Most People Miss

> **Quick answer:** Salary sacrifice means your pension contribution is
> deducted from your gross salary before tax and National Insurance are
> calculated, rather than from your net pay. Because National Insurance
> is never applied to the sacrificed amount, you keep money that a
> standard "relief at source" pension contribution would have lost to
> NI. On a £3,000/year contribution, that's typically an extra £240-£360
> staying in the pension (or back in your pay) compared with a
> non-sacrificed contribution, depending on your NI rate band.

Auto-enrolment gets your pension started; salary sacrifice is the optional next step that makes the same contribution cost you less.

## How is salary sacrifice different from a normal pension contribution?

With a standard contribution, your employer deducts your pension amount from your pay after National Insurance has already been calculated on your full salary, then adds basic-rate tax relief. With salary sacrifice, you and your employer agree to reduce your contractual salary by the contribution amount, and your employer pays that amount straight into your pension instead — before NI is ever calculated on it.

- Gross salary: £35,000
- Pension contribution: £3,000/year (8.6% of salary)
- Employee NI rate: 8%
→ NI saved by sacrificing instead of contributing normally: £3,000 × 8% = £240/year
→ Some employers also pass on their own NI saving (13.8% employer rate) as an extra pension top-up, which can roughly double the benefit

## Does salary sacrifice reduce your take-home pay the same as a normal contribution?

Your take-home pay drops by a similar cash amount either way, since the contribution leaves your pay packet regardless of the mechanism — but salary sacrifice means less of that drop is "wasted" on NI you'd otherwise have paid on money that was never going to reach your bank account anyway. The pension itself ends up larger for the same net cost to you.

## When is salary sacrifice not worth it?

A lower official salary can occasionally affect salary-linked benefits that use your pre-sacrifice figure as a reference, such as some mortgage affordability assessments, statutory maternity pay calculations, or life insurance multiples tied to salary. Check with your HR or benefits team whether your specific entitlements use pre- or post-sacrifice salary before assuming the saving is pure upside.

## How do you find out if your employer offers it?

Not every UK employer runs a salary sacrifice scheme — ask your payroll or HR team directly, since it isn't always advertised alongside standard auto-enrolment paperwork. If it's available, switching is usually a short form, not a renegotiation of your contract terms beyond the pension clause itself.
