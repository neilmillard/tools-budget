# Debt Consolidation Loans vs Balance Transfer Cards: Which Costs Less?

> **Quick answer:** A 0% balance transfer card is usually cheaper if you
> can realistically clear the debt within the promotional period
> (typically 12-28 months) and qualify for a large enough limit. A
> consolidation loan is usually better for larger debts, longer
> repayment needs, or when you want a fixed monthly payment you can't
> be tempted to skip. On £6,000 of debt, a 0% card cleared in 24 months
> costs £0 in interest (plus any transfer fee); a consolidation loan at
> 8% APR over the same period costs roughly £500 in interest.

Both are about moving existing debt somewhere cheaper — the right choice depends on the size of the debt and how fast you can realistically clear it, not which product sounds more serious.

## How much does a 0% balance transfer actually save versus a loan?

- Debt: £6,000
- Balance transfer card: 0% for 24 months, 3% transfer fee
- Consolidation loan: 8% APR, 24-month term
→ Balance transfer total cost: £6,000 × 3% fee = £180, plus £0 interest = £180 total
→ Loan total cost: approximately £500 in interest over 24 months
→ Difference: roughly £320 in favour of the balance transfer, assuming the full balance is cleared before the 0% period ends

The saving only holds if the debt is genuinely paid off within the promotional window — miss that deadline and the card reverts to a standard APR, often higher than the loan rate it was being compared against.

## When does a loan actually work out cheaper or safer?

A loan tends to win when the debt is larger than realistic card limits allow, when the repayment timeline genuinely needs longer than a typical 0% promotional period, or when a fixed required monthly payment is useful discipline — a loan doesn't let the balance quietly grow back if you're tempted to spend on the newly-freed card. Mixing new spending onto a transferred card while still repaying the transferred balance is one of the most common ways a consolidation plan backfires.

## What fees should you check before choosing either option?

- Balance transfer fee (typically 1-5% of the transferred amount, charged upfront)
- Loan arrangement or early repayment fees, if you expect to clear it faster than scheduled
- The card's standard APR after the 0% period ends, in case the balance isn't fully cleared in time

These fees can turn an apparently cheaper option into the more expensive one once the full picture is in.

## How do you decide which method actually fits your situation?

Add up your total debt and your realistic monthly repayment capacity in the [Budget Planner](/tools/budget/) first — the right tool depends entirely on whether that combination fits inside a card's 0% window or needs a longer loan term to clear without strain.

*The 8% APR and 3% transfer fee used here are illustrative for comparison only; actual rates, fees, and available limits depend on your credit profile and the specific lender or card provider. This is not personal financial advice.*
