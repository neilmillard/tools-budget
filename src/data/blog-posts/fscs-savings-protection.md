# Are Your Savings Protected? Understanding FSCS Protection

> **Quick answer:** The Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS)
> protects up to £85,000 per person, per authorised UK bank or building
> society, if that institution fails. The limit applies per banking
> licence, not per bank brand — several well-known "different" banks
> share a single licence, so £85,000 spread across two accounts that
> share a licence is not £170,000 of protection, it's still £85,000.
> Anything held above the limit at a single licence is at risk if that
> institution collapses.

Most people assume "my money's in a bank, it's safe" without checking what that protection actually covers, or where its edges are. For anyone holding meaningful savings, particularly a sinking fund or emergency fund sitting in cash, this is worth five minutes to check properly.

## What exactly does the £85,000 limit cover?

FSCS protects deposits (current accounts, savings accounts, cash ISAs) held with any bank, building society or credit union authorised by the UK's Prudential Regulation Authority, up to £85,000 per eligible person per authorised firm. Joint accounts are protected up to £170,000 combined (£85,000 per named account holder).

- Savings held at Bank A: £60,000 → fully protected (under £85,000 limit)
- Savings held at Bank B: £95,000 → £85,000 protected, £10,000 at risk if Bank B fails

## Why might two "different" banks actually share one licence?

Many UK banking brands operate under a single shared authorisation — a group that owns several household-name savings brands may only hold one FSCS protection limit across all of them combined, not one each. This is the single most common way people accidentally lose protection: assuming two different-sounding brand names means two separate £85,000 limits.

Before splitting savings across banks to stay under the limit, check each institution's FSCS status directly (the FSCS website has a bank-by-bank lookup) — the protection is per licence, and the brand name on the app isn't a reliable guide to which licence sits behind it.

## What should you do if your savings are above £85,000 at one institution?

Split balances across genuinely separate licences, keeping each institution's total under £85,000. This is standard practice, not excessive caution, for anyone holding a large emergency fund, a house deposit, or proceeds from a property sale in cash.
