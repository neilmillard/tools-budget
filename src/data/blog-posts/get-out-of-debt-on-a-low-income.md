# How to Get Out of Debt on a Low Income (UK)

> **Quick answer:** On a low income, deal with priority debts first (rent
> or mortgage, council tax, energy, court fines), because missing those has
> the most serious consequences. Then make sure you're getting every benefit
> and discount you're entitled to. Get **free** debt advice from StepChange,
> National Debtline, Citizens Advice or MoneyHelper. They can arrange
> reduced payments, a 60-day Breathing Space from interest and enforcement,
> a debt management plan, or a Debt Relief Order if you have very little
> spare money. Never pay a company for debt help, and never take a new loan
> to pay off old ones without advice.

January is when the Christmas bills arrive and searches for debt help peak. If your income barely covers the essentials, the usual advice ("throw every spare pound at the smallest debt") doesn't help much when there's no spare pound. This guide is for that situation.

## Which debts should you pay first?

UK debt advisers split debts into two groups.

**Priority debts.** Missing these can lead to losing your home, having your energy cut off, bailiffs, or court action:

- Rent or mortgage, and secured loans
- Council tax
- Gas and electricity
- Court fines, child maintenance, TV licence
- Income tax or VAT owed to HMRC

**Non-priority debts.** These are serious, but the consequences come more slowly and can be negotiated:

- Credit and store cards, overdrafts
- Personal loans, catalogues, buy now pay later
- Money owed to friends and family

Pay priority debts in full where you can, even if it means paying less on non-priority debts for a while. Creditors and debt advisers expect you to do this.

## How can you increase your income when money is tight?

Before cutting further, check that money isn't being left unclaimed:

- **Benefits check:** use a free benefits calculator (Turn2us or entitledto) to see if you qualify for Universal Credit, Council Tax Reduction or other support.
- **Social tariffs:** cheaper broadband and phone deals for people on certain benefits.
- **Energy help:** ask your supplier about the Warm Home Discount and hardship funds.
- **Water:** most water companies run discounted tariffs for low-income households.

## What help is available if you can't afford your debt payments?

Free, regulated debt advice is the most important step. Advisers can:

- **Arrange Breathing Space:** 60 days in which most creditors must freeze interest and charges and pause enforcement while you get advice.
- **Negotiate reduced or token payments** with non-priority creditors.
- **Set up a debt management plan:** one affordable monthly payment shared between your creditors.
- **Help you apply for a Debt Relief Order (DRO):** for people with debts of up to £50,000, less than £75 a month spare after essentials, and few assets. The debts are usually written off after 12 months, and there's no application fee.

### Worked Example

- Monthly income (wages + Universal Credit): £1,450
- Essential costs (rent, council tax, energy, food, travel): £1,390
- Non-priority debts: £9,200 across two credit cards and a catalogue
- Assets: no savings, no car
→ Spare income: £60/month · Under the £75 DRO limit, so an adviser would likely discuss a Debt Relief Order

At £60 a month, paying off £9,200 would take more than 12 years even with interest frozen. That's why a formal solution can make more sense than trying to "budget harder". It's a decision to make with an adviser, because a DRO affects your credit file for six years.

## Should you take a loan to pay off your debts?

Usually not on a low income. Adverts for "a loan to pay off credit cards" are among the most heavily advertised money searches in the UK, because lenders make good money from them. Consolidation can work for someone with a strong credit score and spare income, but if your budget is already stretched it often means a longer debt, and the cards get used again. Get advice first.

## What if you have a little spare money each month?

Once priority debts are covered and you have a small surplus, pick a payoff order and stick to it. See [Debt Snowball vs. Debt Avalanche](/blog/debt-snowball-vs-avalanche/). As the debts clear, your [credit score](/blog/understanding-your-credit-score/) improves too.

*Debt Relief Order limits and Breathing Space rules are as set by the Insolvency Service and the government at the time of writing. Check with a free adviser for your situation. The worked example is illustrative. This is not financial advice. Free debt advice: StepChange, National Debtline, Citizens Advice, MoneyHelper.*
