# Investment Fees: How a 1% Platform Fee Quietly Costs You Tens of Thousands

> **Quick answer:** A 1% annual fee doesn't cost you 1% of your returns —
> it compounds against your balance every year, which means it costs far
> more over decades than the headline number suggests. On a £100,000
> portfolio growing at 7% a year for 30 years, a 1% total fee versus a
> 0.2% fee is the difference between roughly £571,000 and £744,000 at
> the end — over £170,000 lost to fees on the same investment, same
> contributions, same market return.

Fees are one of the only things about investing you can control with certainty. Market returns are unknown in advance; the fee you're paying is written down in a document today.

## Why does a "small" 1% fee cost so much over time?

Because the fee is taken every single year, including on the growth the fee itself prevented — it compounds in reverse. A fee isn't a one-off cost; it's a permanent drag applied annually to a growing balance.

- Starting portfolio: £100,000
- Annual contribution: £0 (lump sum, for clarity)
- Gross annual return: 7%
- Time horizon: 30 years

| Total annual fee | Net annual return | Value after 30 years |
|---|---|---|
| 0.2% (typical low-cost index tracker + platform fee) | 6.8% | ~£744,000 |
| 1.0% (typical actively managed fund + platform fee) | 6.0% | ~£571,000 |

→ Difference from the 0.8 percentage-point fee gap: roughly £173,000, on identical contributions and identical gross market performance.

## What are you actually being charged for?

Two separate charges usually stack on top of each other, and both matter:

- **Fund fee (Ongoing Charges Figure / OCF)** — what the fund manager charges to run the fund itself. A passive index tracker typically runs 0.05%-0.25%; an actively managed fund typically runs 0.75%-1.5%.
- **Platform fee** — what your investment platform charges to hold the fund for you, typically 0.15%-0.45% a year, sometimes capped at a flat fee above a certain balance.

Both are usually quoted separately and need to be added together to see your real total annual cost — a platform's "low-fee" marketing sometimes only refers to one half of what you're actually paying.

## How do you actually reduce what you're paying?

Check your platform's fee schedule and your specific fund's OCF — both are published, usually in a factsheet or key investor document. For most long-term investors, a low-cost, broadly diversified index tracker held on a platform with a low or flat platform fee delivers the majority of a portfolio's long-run return without paying actively-managed pricing for it.
