# The Richest Man in Babylon: The Seven Cures, With Modern UK Tools

> **Quick answer:** *The Richest Man in Babylon* is a set of money parables
> by George S. Clason, first published as pamphlets in 1926. Its core lesson
> is "a part of all you earn is yours to keep": save at least a tenth of
> your income before spending anything, control your spending, invest
> what you save, avoid risky schemes, own your home, plan for old age, and
> increase your earning power. These are the "seven cures for a lean
> purse". A hundred years later they map closely onto modern UK tools:
> automatic standing orders, budgets, ISAs, pensions and a repayment
> mortgage.

The book's language is old-fashioned and its setting is ancient, but the advice has outlasted almost every money book written since. That's why it's the "ancient wisdom" half of Helpful Money's "Ancient wisdom. Modern tools." line. Here's the whole book in one page, with what each idea looks like in practice today.

## What is The Richest Man in Babylon about?

The story follows Arkad, a scribe who becomes the richest man in Babylon. When his friends ask how, he explains that he learned to keep part of everything he earned and to put that money to work. Later chapters cover the "five laws of gold", a moneylender's lessons about lending to friends, and a slave who earns his freedom by paying off his debts. Every chapter makes the same point: wealth comes from steady habits, not luck.

## What are the five laws of gold?

1. Gold comes gladly, and in growing amounts, to anyone who saves at least a tenth of their earnings for their future.
2. Gold works diligently for a wise owner who finds it profitable employment, multiplying like flocks in the field.
3. Gold stays under the protection of an owner who invests it on the advice of people experienced in handling it.
4. Gold slips away from someone who invests in businesses or purposes they don't understand.
5. Gold flees from someone who chases impossible returns, follows the advice of tricksters, or trusts their own inexperience.

Laws 4 and 5 read like a description of modern crypto and "guaranteed return" scams.

## Does the 10% rule still work?

Yes, and compounding makes it more powerful than it looks.

### Worked Example

- Take-home pay: £2,200/month
- Kept back (Cure 1): 10%, £220/month, by standing order on payday
- Invested at: 5% a year, compounded monthly
- Period: 10 years
→ Paid in: £26,400 · Pot after 10 years: £34,162

That's £7,762 earned by your money rather than by you, which is exactly what Arkad means by making your gold multiply. Run your own numbers in the [Investment Calculator](/tools/investment-calculator/). Investments can fall as well as rise, so a 5% average isn't guaranteed.

## Is The Richest Man in Babylon worth reading today?

Yes. It's short, around 150 pages. The parables are easier to remember than most modern advice, and the order of the cures (save, budget, invest, protect, own, insure, earn) matches the journey we use on this site: Debt → Saving → Home → Investing. It is a little dated in places, especially in its attitudes and its faith in moneylenders, so read it for the principles, not the details.
