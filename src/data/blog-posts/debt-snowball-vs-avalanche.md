# Debt Snowball vs. Debt Avalanche: Which Payoff Method Actually Works?

> **Quick answer:** The avalanche method (paying off the highest-interest
> debt first) saves the most money mathematically. The snowball method
> (paying off the smallest balance first) saves less in interest but
> tends to produce more paid-off accounts sooner, which research on
> financial behaviour links to a higher chance of finishing the plan at
> all. On £15,000 across three debts, the difference between the two
> methods is typically a few hundred pounds — the method you'll actually
> stick with is worth more than the one that's technically optimal.

Both methods use the same starting move: pay the minimum on every debt, then throw every spare pound at just one of them. Where they differ is which debt gets that extra pound first.

## How does the avalanche method save the most money?

List every debt by interest rate, highest to lowest, and attack the highest-rate one first with all your spare payment capacity, while paying minimums everywhere else. Once it's cleared, roll that whole payment — minimum plus the extra — onto the next-highest rate.

- Credit card A: £3,000 at 24.9% APR
- Credit card B: £5,000 at 19.9% APR
- Car finance: £7,000 at 8.9% APR

Avalanche order: pay off Card A first (highest rate), then Card B, then the car finance. Because you're eliminating the most expensive interest first, less total interest accrues over the life of the payoff plan than any other order.

## How does the snowball method get more debts to zero faster?

List every debt by balance, smallest to largest, regardless of interest rate, and attack the smallest balance first.

- Credit card A: £3,000
- Credit card B: £5,000
- Car finance: £7,000

Snowball order: Card A first (smallest balance), then Card B, then the car finance — same order in this example, but it won't always match the avalanche order. When it doesn't, snowball clears an account sooner, which behavioural research (notably a 2016 *Journal of Consumer Research* study by Kettle, Trudel, Blanchard and Häubl) links to people being more likely to complete their overall debt payoff plan, not just start it.

## Which method should you actually use?

If the interest-rate gap between your debts is small, use snowball — the interest cost difference is minor, and the early win from clearing a full account keeps momentum going. If the gap is large — a 24.9% card next to an 8.9% car loan — the avalanche method's interest savings become significant enough that it's usually worth the slower start.

- Interest-rate spread across your debts: small (all within a few percentage points) → snowball
- Interest-rate spread across your debts: large (10+ percentage points) → avalanche

Either way, the method that fails is the one you abandon. Map out your actual balances and minimum payments with the [Budget Planner](/tools/budget/) to see how much spare payment capacity you genuinely have each month before committing to either order.

*The behavioural research referenced is Kettle, Trudel, Blanchard & Häubl, "Failure Aversion," Journal of Consumer Research (2016), cited here for its finding on debt-account-closure and completion rates, not as financial advice specific to any individual's circumstances.*
