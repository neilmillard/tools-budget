# When Will My Mortgage Be Paid Off? How to Work Out Your Date

> **Quick answer:** On a UK repayment mortgage, your payoff date is the end
> of your mortgage term, as long as you keep making the normal payments and
> the rate stays the same. To find it, take your current balance, rate and
> remaining term from your latest annual statement or lender app, and put
> them into a mortgage overpayment calculator. Overpaying brings the date
> forward. In the early years most of each payment is interest, so the
> balance falls more slowly than people expect.

Plenty of people know their monthly payment to the penny but couldn't say how much they still owe or when it will end. Both are easy to find, and once you know them the mortgage stops feeling like a permanent fixture.

## How much have I paid off my mortgage so far?

Your lender sends an annual statement showing the outstanding balance, and most lenders show it live in their app or online account. "How much have I paid off" is simply the original loan minus that balance. It is **not** the total of the payments you have made, because a large share of those payments went on interest.

### Worked Example

Here is how a typical mortgage splits in its first ten years, calculated with standard repayment-mortgage amortisation:

- Original loan: £200,000
- Rate: 4.5%
- Term: 25 years
- Monthly payment: £1,111.66
→ After 5 years: £66,700 paid in total, but only £24,284 off the balance (£42,416 was interest). Balance: £175,716
→ After 10 years: £54,683 off the balance (£78,717 interest). Balance: £145,317

So after five years of payments, the balance is down by only about an eighth. That's normal. The interest is charged on the outstanding balance, so it's largest at the start and shrinks as the balance falls. In the final years almost all of each payment goes on the loan itself.

## When will my mortgage be paid off if I keep paying as normal?

If you make every payment and the rate doesn't change, the mortgage ends at the end of its term: 25 years after it started in the example above. Three things change that date:

- **Remortgaging to a new term.** Many people quietly reset to a fresh 25 years when they remortgage. That lowers the payment but pushes the payoff date back.
- **Rate changes.** On a repayment mortgage the lender usually adjusts your payment to keep the original end date, so a rate change alters the payment rather than the date.
- **Overpayments.** These reduce the balance directly, and most lenders let you choose between a shorter term or a lower payment afterwards. Choosing the shorter term is what brings the date forward.

## How much earlier would overpaying get me there?

Same mortgage, with a regular overpayment from the start:

- Original loan: £200,000
- Rate: 4.5%
- Term: 25 years
- Overpayment: £200/month
→ Paid off in 18 years 11 months, 6 years 1 month early · Interest saved: £36,280

You can run the same calculation part-way through a mortgage. Use your current balance and remaining term instead of the original figures. ## What should you check before you overpay?

- **Your overpayment allowance.** Many fixed deals allow about 10% of the balance each year before an early repayment charge applies.
- **Your emergency fund.** Money paid into the mortgage is hard to get back. Build the safety net first.
- **Higher-interest debt.** A credit card at 20%+ should be cleared before a mortgage at 4-5%.
