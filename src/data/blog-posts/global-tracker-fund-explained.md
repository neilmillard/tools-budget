# What Is a Global Tracker Fund? A Plain-English UK Guide

> **Quick answer:** A global tracker fund is one fund that automatically
> holds shares in thousands of companies around the world, in proportion to
> their size, by copying a global stock market index such as the MSCI World
> or the FTSE All-World. It gives you worldwide diversification in a single
> purchase, usually for an ongoing charge of roughly 0.1%-0.3% a year.
> Inside a stocks and shares ISA or pension, it's the simplest core
> holding a long-term UK investor can own.

In [The Simple Portfolio Most People Should Just Use](/blog/simple-portfolio-index-funds/) we argued that most people don't need to pick shares, funds or fund managers. A global tracker is what that looks like in practice: one fund that does the job of many.

## What does a global tracker fund actually hold?

It holds whatever its index holds. The main families of global indices are:

- **MSCI World:** large and medium-sized companies in developed markets only (the US, UK, Europe, Japan and so on), roughly 1,400 companies.
- **MSCI ACWI / FTSE All-World:** the same, plus emerging markets such as China, India and Brazil, a few thousand companies in total.
- **FTSE Global All Cap:** adds smaller companies as well, around 10,000 holdings.

Because each company is held in proportion to its market value, the US makes up well over half of most global trackers, and the largest technology companies are the biggest single holdings. That's not a fund manager's bet. It's what the world's stock market looks like.

## Is a global tracker fund the same as an index fund?

A global tracker is one type of index fund. "Index fund" covers anything that copies an index. A FTSE 100 tracker, for example, holds only 100 large UK companies. A global tracker copies a worldwide index, so you aren't relying on a single country.

Trackers come as **funds** (OEICs/unit trusts, bought at one price per day) or **ETFs** (traded on the stock exchange like a share). For a monthly investor either works. The total cost matters more than the wrapper.

## How much does a global tracker cost, and does it matter?

The fund's ongoing charge (OCF) for a global tracker is typically about 0.1%-0.3% a year. A platform fee is often charged on top, which we cover in [Investment Fees](/blog/investment-platform-fees/). Small percentages compound into large sums.

### Worked Example

- Monthly investment: £200
- Period: 20 years
- Market return before costs: 5% a year
- Total yearly costs: 0.2% (tracker + low-cost platform) vs 1% (typical active fund)
→ Pot at 0.2%: £80,335 · Pot at 1%: £73,355 · Difference: £6,980 on £48,000 paid in

Nothing else changes between the two pots. The higher-cost fund has to beat the market by 0.8% a year, every year, just to match the tracker, and most active funds don't manage that over 20 years.

## What are the downsides of a global tracker?

- **It falls when markets fall.** In a bad year a global tracker can drop 20-30%. It's for money you won't need for at least five years.
- **It's concentrated in the US and big tech**, because they make up so much of the world market.
- **It holds no bonds**, so on its own it's 100% shares. If you want smoother returns, add a bond fund as described in the simple portfolio post.

## Where should you hold a global tracker in the UK?

Inside a **stocks and shares ISA** (growth and dividends tax-free) or a **pension** (tax relief on the way in). See [Using Your ISA Allowance](/blog/using-isa-allowance/). Investing comes after the Debt and Saving stages, so clear expensive debt and build an emergency fund first.

*Index descriptions are simplified and holdings counts change over time. Check the fund's factsheet for its exact index, charges and holdings. Worked figures use monthly compounding at a constant return, which real markets never deliver. Investments can fall as well as rise. This is general education, not financial advice or a recommendation of any fund.*
