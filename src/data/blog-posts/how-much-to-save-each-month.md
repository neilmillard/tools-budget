# How Much Should I Save Each Month? A Simple UK Method

> **Quick answer:** As a general rule, aim to save at least 10% of your
> take-home pay, working up to 20% once debts are under control. For a
> specific goal, divide what you need by the number of months until you
> need it. Saving £10,000 in 3 years means about £278 a month, or about
> £262 a month if the savings earn 4% interest. Set up the amount as a
> standing order on payday so it happens before you spend anything.

The last week of the year is a natural time to set the numbers for the next one. There are two questions here: how much should you save in general, and how much do you need to save for a particular thing? They have different answers.

## What percentage of my income should I save?

- **10% as a minimum.** It's the oldest rule in personal finance and still works: see [The Psychology of the 10% Rule](/blog/psychology-of-10-percent-rule/).
- **20% as a target.** The 50/30/20 rule puts 20% towards savings and debt repayment. See [What the 50-30-20 Rule Actually Looks Like on a Real Salary](/blog/what-the-50-30-20-rule-looks-like/).
- **Workplace pension contributions are on top.** They're taken before your take-home pay, so they don't count towards these figures.

If you have credit card or other high-interest debt, extra money usually goes further paying that off than sitting in savings. Keep a small starter emergency fund, then attack the debt.

## How much do I need to save each month for a goal?

The simple version: **goal ÷ months = monthly saving.** Interest makes the real number slightly lower, but the simple version is close enough to plan with, and the interest becomes a bonus.

### Worked Example

- Goal: £10,000 (for example, an emergency fund or a car)
- Time: 3 years (36 months)
- Savings rate: 4% a year, compounded monthly
→ Without interest: £277.78/month · With 4% interest: £261.91/month

- Goal: £5,000
- Time: 2 years (24 months)
- Savings rate: 4% a year
→ Without interest: £208.33/month · With 4% interest: £200.46/month

If the monthly figure is more than your budget can handle, you have three options: extend the deadline, reduce the goal, or find the money elsewhere in your spending. Change the plan before you start, not halfway through.

## What should I be saving for first?

Follow the journey in order:

1. **A starter emergency fund**, then pay off expensive debt (Debt stage).
2. **A full emergency fund** of 3-6 months' essential costs. See [Your Financial Safety Net](/blog/your-financial-safety-net/).
3. **Sinking funds** for costs you know are coming, like car repairs, Christmas and holidays. See [Sinking Funds: The Envelope Method](/blog/sinking-funds-envelope-method/).
4. **Bigger goals**, like a house deposit or long-term investing.

## How do I make sure I actually save it?

Automate it. Set a standing order for the day after payday, into a separate account you don't see every day. Saving what's left at the end of the month rarely works, because there rarely is anything left. The money you never see in your current account is the money you don't miss.

*Worked figures use monthly compounding at a constant rate. Real savings rates change, and interest may be taxable outside an ISA above your Personal Savings Allowance. This is not financial advice.*
