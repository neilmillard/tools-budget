# Fixed vs Tracker Mortgage: Which Should You Choose?

> **Quick answer:** A fixed-rate mortgage locks your interest rate for a
> set period (typically 2, 5 or 10 years), so your payment never changes
> even if the Bank of England base rate rises. A tracker moves directly
> with the base rate plus a fixed margin, so your payment can fall as
> well as rise. Fixed suits people who want certainty and are on a tight
> monthly budget; tracker suits people who can absorb payment swings and
> expect rates to fall over their term. On a £200,000 mortgage, a 1
> percentage point rate difference moves the monthly payment by roughly
> £95-£115 depending on term.

Every UK mortgage deal eventually comes down to this choice, and the "right" answer depends more on your own cash-flow tolerance than on guessing where rates go next.

## How much does a 1% rate difference actually cost per month?

- Mortgage balance: £200,000
- Term remaining: 25 years
- Rate A (fixed): 4.5%
- Rate B (tracker, after a rate move): 5.5%
→ Monthly payment at 4.5%: approximately £1,111
→ Monthly payment at 5.5%: approximately £1,228
→ Difference: roughly £117/month, or £1,404/year, for a single percentage point move

That's the number a tracker borrower is exposed to with every base rate change — in either direction.

## What does a fixed rate actually protect you from?

A fixed rate doesn't make your mortgage cheaper; it makes it predictable. If the base rate rises twice during your fixed term, a fixed borrower pays the same as before while a tracker borrower on the same starting rate absorbs both increases immediately. The trade-off is the reverse during rate cuts — the fixed borrower doesn't benefit until their deal ends and they remortgage, while the tracker borrower's payment drops straight away.

## When does a tracker mortgage make more sense than a fix?

A tracker tends to make more sense when:

- Your budget has headroom to absorb a payment rise of £100-£200/month without missing other goals
- You expect to remortgage or sell within a shorter window than the typical fixed term
- Base rates are judged more likely to fall than rise over your expected holding period

None of these are certainties — a tracker is a bet on rate direction, while a fix is a bet on your own need for certainty.

## What should you check before switching?

Compare the actual monthly payment difference for your own balance and term, not someone else's average, and check whether your current deal carries an early repayment charge for switching before it ends — that charge can erase several years of savings from an early switch.
