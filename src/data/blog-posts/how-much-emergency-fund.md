# How Much Emergency Fund Do You Actually Need? (UK)

> **Quick answer:** Most UK households need an emergency fund of three to
> six months of **essential** costs: housing, council tax, energy, food,
> transport, insurance and minimum debt payments, not your full income or
> lifestyle spending. Aim for three months with a secure job and a second
> income in the household. Aim for six or more if you're self-employed, the
> only earner, or have dependants. While you're paying off expensive debt,
> keep a starter fund of about £1,000. Keep it in an easy-access savings
> account, separate from your current account.

The New Year is when people decide to "sort out their savings". The emergency fund comes first, because it protects every other goal. Without one, a broken boiler or a gap between jobs goes straight onto a credit card.

## How do you work out your emergency fund number?

Add up what you'd **have to** pay each month if your income stopped. Leave out anything you could cancel or pause: subscriptions, eating out, holidays, extra pension or savings contributions.

### Worked Example

| Essential cost | Per month |
|:--|--:|
| Rent or mortgage | £850 |
| Council tax | £150 |
| Energy | £140 |
| Water | £40 |
| Food | £300 |
| Transport | £150 |
| Phone and broadband | £60 |
| Insurance | £60 |
| Minimum debt payments | £100 |
| **Total essential costs** | **£1,850** |

→ 3-month fund: £5,550 · 6-month fund: £11,100

Building the 3-month fund over 18 months means saving about £308 a month.

## Should your emergency fund be 3 or 6 months?

Lean towards **3 months** if:

- your job is secure and in demand,
- there are two incomes in the household and either could cover the essentials,
- you have no dependants.

Lean towards **6 months or more** if:

- you're self-employed, a contractor or paid by commission,
- you're the only earner, or have children or other dependants,
- you own an older home or car that could need a big repair,
- your industry takes a long time to find work in.

## How much emergency fund do you need while paying off debt?

Keep a **starter fund of around £1,000**, or one month of essential costs, and put everything else into the debt. Once expensive debt is cleared, build the full 3-6 months. Without the starter fund, the first emergency usually goes straight back onto the card you're trying to clear.

## Where should you keep your emergency fund?

In an **easy-access (instant-access) savings account** or easy-access cash ISA, where you can withdraw the same day without a penalty. Not in shares, which can fall just when you need the money, and not in a fixed-term account you can't get into. Keep it at a different bank from your current account so it isn't too tempting to dip into, and check it's covered by [FSCS protection](/blog/fscs-savings-protection/).

For when to use it and how to refill it, see [Your Financial Safety Net](/blog/your-financial-safety-net/). Money for costs you can see coming, like Christmas or car servicing, doesn't belong in the emergency fund. That's what [sinking funds](/blog/sinking-funds-envelope-method/) are for.
