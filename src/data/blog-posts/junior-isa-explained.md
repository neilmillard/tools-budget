# Junior ISAs Explained: Saving for Your Child's Future

> **Quick answer:** A Junior ISA (JISA) lets you save or invest up to
> £9,000 per tax year, tax-free, for a child under 18. It comes in cash
> and stocks & shares versions, can be opened by a parent or legal
> guardian, and the money becomes the child's own property at 18 — they
> can access it and spend it however they choose, not just on anything
> the contributor had in mind. £100/month invested from birth at an
> average 6% return becomes roughly £33,700 by age 18.

A JISA is simple in structure but has one feature worth understanding before you open one: once money goes in, it belongs to the child, not to you.

## How much could a regular contribution actually grow to by 18?

- Monthly contribution: £100
- Starting age: birth
- Time horizon: 18 years
- Average annual return (stocks & shares JISA): 6%
→ Total contributed: £21,600
→ Estimated value at 18: approximately £33,700
→ Growth contributed by compounding: roughly £12,100, more than half the total contributions themselves given the long time horizon

The earlier you start, the more of the final total comes from growth rather than your own contributions — an 18-year horizon is long enough for compounding to do real work.

## What happens to the money when the child turns 18?

At 18, a JISA automatically converts to an adult ISA and the child gains full control — they can withdraw it, spend it, or keep investing it, entirely at their own discretion. Parents and grandparents sometimes assume the money will go toward university fees or a house deposit, but legally there's no way to restrict what an 18-year-old does with their own ISA. If that control question matters to your family, a Junior ISA may not be the only vehicle worth considering alongside it.

## Cash or stocks & shares for a child's JISA?

The same time-horizon logic from any ISA decision applies, usually more strongly: a newborn's JISA has an 18-year minimum horizon before the child can even access it, which is long enough to justify a stocks & shares JISA for most of the contribution in most cases. A JISA opened for a teenager with only a year or two left before access has much less time to recover from a market dip, and a cash JISA — or a cash/stocks split — becomes more defensible as the 18th birthday approaches.

## How does the £9,000 annual JISA limit work alongside your own ISA allowance?

The JISA allowance is separate from your own personal £20,000 ISA allowance — contributing to a child's JISA doesn't use up any of your own. Anyone can contribute to a child's JISA (parents, grandparents, other family), but the combined total across all contributors can't exceed £9,000 in a tax year.
