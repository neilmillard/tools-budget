# Cash ISA vs Stocks & Shares ISA: Which Should You Actually Use?

> **Quick answer:** A Cash ISA pays interest like a savings account and
> carries no risk of losing the capital you put in — use it for money
> you need within roughly 5 years. A Stocks & Shares ISA invests in the
> market, carries short-term risk, but has historically outgrown cash
> over longer periods — use it for money you won't need for 5+ years.
> £10,000 left in cash at 4% for 10 years becomes about £14,800; the
> same £10,000 in a global index fund averaging 7% becomes about
> £19,700 — but only if you can tolerate the falls along the way.

Both sit inside the same £20,000 annual ISA allowance, and you can split that allowance between them — this isn't an either/or decision at the account-type level, it's about matching each pound to its own time horizon.

## How much difference does the growth rate actually make over time?

- Starting amount: £10,000
- Time horizon: 10 years
- Cash ISA, average rate: 4%
- Stocks & Shares ISA, average return: 7%
→ Cash ISA after 10 years: approximately £14,800
→ Stocks & Shares ISA after 10 years: approximately £19,700
→ Difference: roughly £4,900, assuming the market return is achieved smoothly, which it never is in practice

## Why does the market return come with a catch the cash return doesn't?

The 7% is an average, not a guaranteed path — a Stocks & Shares ISA can fall 15-20% in a single bad year before recovering over the following years. A Cash ISA's 4% doesn't fall; it just sits there, visibly smaller in the compounding comparison but never negative in cash terms. The extra return from investing is compensation for tolerating that variability, not a free upgrade.

## Which one should your emergency fund sit in?

Money you might need at short notice — an emergency fund, a house deposit due within a couple of years, a known upcoming expense — belongs in a Cash ISA or easy-access savings, not invested, because a market dip at exactly the wrong moment can force you to sell at a loss to cover something urgent. The [Budget Planner](/tools/budget/) can help size that fund before deciding how much is actually left over to invest.

## Can you hold both types of ISA in the same tax year?

Yes — the £20,000 allowance can be split across a Cash ISA, a Stocks & Shares ISA, and other ISA types in any combination you choose within the same tax year, as long as the total doesn't exceed £20,000. A common approach is to fill a cash buffer first, then direct the remaining allowance into a Stocks & Shares ISA for longer-term goals.
