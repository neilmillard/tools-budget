# Paid Off Your Mortgage Early? The UK Checklist for the Final Payment

> **Quick answer:** Before you pay off a UK mortgage early, ask your lender
> for a redemption statement so you know the exact figure, including any
> early repayment charge and exit fee. After the final payment, check that
> the lender's charge has been removed from your title at HM Land Registry,
> tell your buildings insurer the lender no longer has an interest, cancel
> the direct debit, and give the old monthly payment a new job straight
> away so it doesn't disappear into everyday spending.

Clearing the mortgage is the biggest single milestone in the Home stage of the journey. Most of the work is done by the time you make the last payment, but there are a few loose ends that lenders don't always tie up for you. This is the checklist we'd want in front of us.

## Can you pay off a mortgage early in the UK?

Yes. Every UK mortgage can be repaid early. The question is what it costs. Most fixed and tracker deals carry an **early repayment charge (ERC)** for the length of the deal, usually a percentage of the amount repaid that steps down each year (for example 5%, 4%, 3%...). Most lenders also let you overpay a set amount each year, commonly 10% of the balance, without triggering the charge.

That gives you three routes to an early finish:

1. **Overpay within the allowance** every year until the balance is gone. No charges.
2. **Wait for the deal to end**, then clear the remainder while on the lender's standard variable rate, when ERCs no longer apply.
3. **Pay it all off mid-deal** and accept the ERC, if the interest you save outweighs it.

## What should you do before the final payment?

1. **Ask for a redemption statement.** It shows the exact amount needed to clear the loan on a given date, including daily interest, any ERC, and any exit or "deeds release" fee. Pay that figure, not the balance on your last annual statement.
2. **Check the ERC dates.** If the fixed rate ends in a few weeks, waiting can save hundreds or thousands of pounds.
3. **Keep your emergency fund intact.** Don't empty your savings to make the final payment. Once money is in the house it can't easily come back out. See [Your Financial Safety Net](/blog/your-financial-safety-net/).

## What should you do after you've paid off your mortgage?

1. **Get written confirmation** from the lender that the mortgage is redeemed. Keep it with your property papers.
2. **Check the Land Registry title.** For registered land, the lender should send a discharge to HM Land Registry, which removes their charge. A few weeks later, download a copy of your title register for a small fee and check the charge has gone.
3. **Ask about old paper deeds.** Most titles are now held electronically, but some lenders still hold original paper documents. Ask for them back.
4. **Update your buildings insurance.** The lender is often recorded as having an interest in the policy. Tell your insurer it has been removed.
5. **Cancel the direct debit** once the lender confirms the final payment cleared, and look out for any small refund of overpaid interest.
6. **Review mortgage-linked life cover.** Decreasing term cover taken out to clear the mortgage may no longer be needed, but check first whether your family still relies on it for other things.
7. **Check your credit file** a month or two later to see that the mortgage shows as settled.

## What should you do with the money you used to pay the mortgage?

This is where most of the long-term value is. In the worked example above, £1,083.75 a month (the normal payment plus the overpayment) is suddenly free. If nobody gives it a job, [lifestyle creep](/blog/lifestyle-creep/) will. Redirect it on the first payday after redemption, into pensions, ISAs and long-term investments.
