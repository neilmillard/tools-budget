# Saving for a House Deposit: Lifetime ISA, Cash ISA or Savings Account?

> **Quick answer:** For most UK first-time buyers aged 18-39 buying a home
> worth £450,000 or less, a Lifetime ISA is the best place for the first
> £4,000 a year of a house deposit, because the government adds a 25% bonus
> (up to £1,000 a year). The account must be open for 12 months before you
> use it to buy. Anything above £4,000 a year, or money you might need for
> something else, belongs in a cash ISA or easy-access savings account.
> Keep a deposit you'll need within about five years in cash, not shares.

The deposit is the first big hurdle of the Home stage. It decides whether you can buy at all, and its size also decides the mortgage rates you're offered. Getting the most out of every pound you save for it matters.

## How much deposit do you need to buy a house in the UK?

The minimum is usually **5%** of the purchase price. A bigger deposit gets better rates, with noticeable steps at 10%, 15% and 25% (lenders call these loan-to-value bands: 90%, 85%, 75%). On top of the deposit, budget for:

- Solicitor/conveyancing fees
- Survey
- Mortgage arrangement fees (sometimes added to the loan)
- Stamp Duty, if the price is above the first-time buyer threshold
- Moving costs and the first bits of furniture

Our [Affordability Calculator](/tools/afford/) shows what you could borrow, which tells you what price range, and therefore what deposit, to aim for. [How to Know if You Can Afford a Mortgage Before You Apply](/blog/know-if-you-can-afford-mortgage/) goes through the lender checks in detail.

## How does a Lifetime ISA work for a house deposit?

- You can open one if you're aged **18 to 39**, and pay in until you turn 50.
- You can pay in up to **£4,000 a year**. It counts towards your overall £20,000 ISA allowance.
- The government adds a **25% bonus**, up to £1,000 a year.
- For a house purchase, you must be a **first-time buyer**, the property must cost **£450,000 or less**, and the account must have been open for at least **12 months**.
- If you take money out for any other reason (apart from at 60 or later, or terminal illness), there is a **25% withdrawal charge**. That charge takes back the bonus and a bit of your own money.

Two people buying together can each use their own Lifetime ISA, which doubles the bonus.

### Worked Example

A couple saving for a deposit on a £250,000 home:

- Each pays £333/month into their own Lifetime ISA (£4,000 a year each)
- Saving period: 3 years
- Government bonus: 25%
→ Paid in: £24,000 together · Bonus: £6,000 · Deposit: £30,000 before interest, 12% of the price

Saving the same £666 a month in an ordinary savings account would leave the couple £6,000 short. That's about nine more months of saving to reach the same deposit, and interest wouldn't come close to closing the gap.

## When is a cash ISA or savings account better?

- **You're over 39**, or the home will cost more than £450,000.
- **You might not buy.** If there's a real chance the money goes on something else, the 25% withdrawal charge makes a Lifetime ISA expensive.
- **You're buying within 12 months** and the Lifetime ISA isn't open yet.
- **You're saving more than £4,000 a year.** Put the extra in a cash ISA or a high-interest easy-access account alongside the Lifetime ISA.

## Should a house deposit be in cash or stocks and shares?

If you plan to buy within about five years, keep it in cash. Stock markets can fall 20-30% in a bad year, and a deposit that shrinks just before you need it can delay the purchase for years. Stocks and shares Lifetime ISAs exist, but they suit retirement saving better than a near-term deposit.

Treat the deposit fund as its own [sinking fund](/blog/sinking-funds-envelope-method/), separate from your emergency fund, so a broken boiler doesn't wipe out your deposit.

*Lifetime ISA limits, bonus and property cap are as set by HMRC at the time of writing. The government has signalled it may reform the Lifetime ISA, so check gov.uk before opening one. Worked figures exclude interest. This is not financial advice.*
