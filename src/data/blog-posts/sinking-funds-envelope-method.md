# Sinking Funds: The Envelope Method for Expenses You Know Are Coming

> **Quick answer:** A sinking fund is a dedicated pot you save into monthly
> for a specific, predictable future expense — car insurance, Christmas,
> a boiler service — so the bill never has to come out of this month's
> cash flow or a credit card. Splitting a £600 annual car insurance
> renewal into £50/month means the money is already there when the
> renewal notice lands, instead of being an "emergency" every single year.

An expense you can see coming a year away isn't an emergency. It only feels like one if you didn't save for it on the way there. That's the gap a sinking fund closes — and it's different from your emergency fund, which exists for the things you genuinely can't predict.

## What's the difference between a sinking fund and an emergency fund?

An emergency fund covers the unpredictable: redundancy, a boiler that dies without warning, an unplanned repair. A sinking fund covers the predictable: things you already know will happen, on a schedule you can mostly guess.

- **Emergency fund** → one pot, for anything, size targeted at 3-6 months of essential spending
- **Sinking funds** → several small pots, each earmarked for one known future expense, sized to that expense's actual cost and timing

Mixing the two causes the most common budgeting failure: raiding the emergency fund every December for Christmas, then feeling like you're perpetually "behind" on your safety net. They're not competing for the same money — they're solving different problems.

## How do you size a sinking fund for a known expense?

Take the expense's total annual cost and divide by the number of months until it's due:

- Car insurance renewal: £600/year → £50/month
- Christmas and gifts: £480/year → £40/month
- Car MOT + service: £360/year → £30/month
- Annual subscriptions (streaming, software): £240/year → £20/month

- Total known annual expenses: £1,680
- Months to save across: 12
→ Monthly sinking-fund contribution needed: £140/month

Once that £140/month is built into the budget as a fixed line item — not a "nice to have" — the December scramble and the surprise insurance renewal both stop being surprises.

## Where should sinking fund money actually sit?

It needs to be separate from your everyday spending account, or it will quietly get spent on something else before the bill arrives. A simple, cheap approach: one easy-access savings account per fund (or one account with sub-pots if your bank supports them), and a standing order that moves the money the day you're paid — not whatever's left over at month end.
