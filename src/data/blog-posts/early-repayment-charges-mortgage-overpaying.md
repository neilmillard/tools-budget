# Early Repayment Charges: When Mortgage Overpaying Actually Costs You

> **Quick answer:** Most UK fixed-rate mortgage deals allow you to
> overpay up to 10% of the outstanding balance per year without penalty
> — overpay beyond that limit during a fixed deal and you can trigger
> an Early Repayment Charge (ERC), typically 1-5% of the amount over the
> limit, which can erase years of interest savings in a single
> overpayment. On a £200,000 mortgage, overpaying £30,000 in one year
> against a 10%/£20,000 allowance and a 3% ERC costs £300 in penalties
> on the excess £10,000 alone.

Overpaying is one of the most effective ways to cut mortgage interest — but only within the allowance your specific deal actually permits.

## How much can you overpay before the penalty allowance runs out?

- Outstanding mortgage balance: £200,000
- Standard overpayment allowance: 10% per year
→ Allowance: £20,000/year without triggering an ERC
- Attempted overpayment: £30,000
- Amount over the allowance: £10,000
- ERC rate on the excess (varies by lender and remaining fixed term): 3%
→ Penalty: £10,000 × 3% = £300

The allowance resets each year of your fixed term and is usually calculated against the balance at the start of that year, not the original loan amount — check your specific lender's mortgage offer document for the exact wording, as "10% of the balance" is common but not universal.

## Does the overpayment allowance apply after your fixed deal ends?

No — once your fixed, tracker, or discount deal period ends and you move to the lender's standard variable rate (or remortgage), the overpayment allowance and any ERC restriction normally disappear with it. The limit is a feature of the deal's early-exit terms, not a permanent rule attached to the mortgage itself.

## Is it ever worth paying the penalty to overpay anyway?

Occasionally — if the interest saved from clearing the balance faster exceeds the ERC itself, and you have no better use for the money (no higher-interest debt, pension allowance already used, no other goal), the overpayment can still be worth it even after the charge. Run the actual numbers rather than assuming; a 3% one-off penalty against years of saved interest at 4-5% can still net out positive on a large enough excess overpayment, but it isn't automatic.

## What should you check before making a large overpayment?

Call your lender or check your mortgage's annual statement for the exact overpayment allowance and ERC percentage before transferring a lump sum — these details aren't always obvious in online banking and vary significantly between lenders and deal types.
