# Budgeting on a Variable Income: The Self-Employed Guide

> **Quick answer:** Budget against your lowest realistic monthly income
> from the last 6-12 months, not your average or your best month — pay
> yourself a fixed "salary" from the business at that baseline level,
> and route anything earned above it into a buffer account that smooths
> out the gaps. On income that ranges from £1,800 to £3,600/month, a
> baseline budget of £1,800 means every month above that funds next
> month's shortfall instead of being spent as if it were permanent.

A fixed monthly budget assumes a fixed monthly income — the first adjustment for variable earners is accepting that the "income" line in a normal budget needs its own buffer system before anything else works.

## How do you actually set a baseline when income swings month to month?

- Last 12 months' income, lowest month: £1,800
- Last 12 months' income, highest month: £3,600
- Last 12 months' income, average month: £2,700
→ Baseline for fixed essential spending: £1,800 (the lowest, not the average)
→ Buffer built from every month above baseline: the difference banked, not spent

Using the average instead of the lowest month feels more generous but guarantees a shortfall in every below-average month — the baseline has to survive the worst realistic month, not the typical one.

## What goes in the buffer account and what doesn't?

The buffer exists specifically to cover essential spending in a low-income month — rent or mortgage, utilities, food, minimum debt payments — not discretionary spending that can simply be skipped when income is down. Treat any month's income above the baseline as "future months' income that arrived early," not as extra money for the current month, until the buffer holds at least one full baseline month's worth of essentials.

## How much buffer do you need before it's actually working?

A buffer covering 1-2 months of baseline essential spending is usually enough to smooth normal income variability for most self-employed earners; income that's highly seasonal or project-based may need 3+ months. This sits alongside, not instead of, a standard emergency fund — the buffer absorbs predictable income lumpiness, while the emergency fund covers unpredictable shocks like losing a client or an unexpected bill.

## Where should tax money sit in this system?

Set aside your estimated Self Assessment tax and National Insurance the moment income arrives, in a separate account you don't touch — treating it as already spent the day you're paid avoids the single most common cash-flow crisis for self-employed earners: a strong income year followed by a tax bill nobody budgeted for.
