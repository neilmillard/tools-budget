# Workplace Pension Auto-Enrolment: What You're Actually Saving

> **Quick answer:** UK auto-enrolment requires a minimum total pension
> contribution of 8% of qualifying earnings — at least 3% from your
> employer and the rest topped up by you and tax relief — automatically
> deducted unless you actively opt out. On a £30,000 salary, that's
> roughly £1,300/year going into a pension you might not have noticed
> starting, on top of whatever your employer contributes separately.
> Opting out forfeits the employer's share entirely — it isn't paid to
> you instead, it simply isn't paid at all.

If you've started a UK job in the last decade, you were very likely auto-enrolled into a workplace pension without applying for it — that's the point of the scheme. Understanding what's actually happening to that money is worth five minutes.

## How does auto-enrolment actually split the 8% minimum?

The legal minimum on qualifying earnings (currently earnings between roughly £6,240 and £50,270 a year) breaks down as:

- Employer minimum contribution: 3%
- Employee contribution: 4%
- Tax relief added automatically: 1%
→ Total minimum going into the pension: 8%

- Qualifying earnings: £30,000 (approximating full salary for simplicity)
- Employer's 3%: £900/year
- Your 4% (before tax relief): £1,200/year, but effectively costs you less than £1,200 in take-home pay because of the tax relief
- Tax relief top-up: £300/year (basic-rate relief added by the government)
→ Total annual contribution: £2,400, of which only £900 came from the employer and roughly £960 came from your net pay

## Why does opting out cost more than it looks like?

Opting out stops your 4% contribution — but it also stops the employer's 3%, which was never yours to redirect elsewhere. It doesn't get paid as extra salary if you opt out; it simply isn't paid. Turning down free money that doesn't cost you anything extra to receive is the single clearest case for staying enrolled, unless a genuine short-term cash-flow emergency makes it unavoidable.

## Should you contribute more than the 8% minimum?

Many employers offer to match contributions above the legal minimum — some will match up to 5% or 6% if you also increase your own contribution, effectively doubling part of your extra saving before it even reaches the market. Check your specific scheme's matching structure; it's usually in your employee handbook or benefits portal, and it's easy to miss because auto-enrolment already happened without you looking.
