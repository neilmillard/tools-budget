# Net Worth Tracking: The One Number That Actually Measures Progress

> **Quick answer:** Net worth is everything you own (savings, investments,
> property, pension) minus everything you owe (mortgage, loans, credit
> cards), and it's a more honest progress measure than income or savings
> balance alone, because it captures debt paydown and investment growth
> in the same figure. Someone earning £80,000 with £40,000 of debt can
> have a lower net worth than someone earning £35,000 with no debt and
> steady savings — income tells you what came in, net worth tells you
> what you've actually kept and built.

Income is easy to compare and easy to obsess over, but it's a flow, not a total. Net worth is the total — the single number that reflects every financial decision you've made up to today, not just this month's payslip.

## Why is net worth a better progress measure than your bank balance?

A bank balance only shows one account, at one moment, and ignores debt entirely. Net worth nets everything together, so paying down £500 of credit card debt shows up as clearly as saving £500 — both move the same number in the same direction, which a bank balance alone can't show.

- Savings and investments: £22,000
- Pension pot: £35,000
- Home equity (property value minus mortgage owed): £60,000
- Credit card and loan balances: −£4,000
→ Net worth: £113,000

Tracked monthly, that single figure captures debt payoff, investment growth, and saving all at once — three separate efforts most people track separately, if they track them at all.

## How often should you actually check it?

Monthly, not daily. Daily tracking mostly measures stock market noise on the investment portion of your net worth, which is unhelpful and can be actively discouraging during a normal market dip. Monthly tracking is frequent enough to catch a trend (net worth flat or falling for three months running is worth investigating) without reacting to volatility that reverses itself within weeks.

## What should you do if your net worth isn't moving?

A flat or falling net worth over several months means one of three things is happening, and the fix is different for each:

- **Spending is outpacing income** → revisit your budget with the [Budget Planner](/tools/budget/) to find where the gap is
- **Debt is growing faster than assets** → prioritise paying down high-interest debt before adding to savings
- **Assets are there but untracked** → a workplace pension or old employer's pension pot is often simply missing from the calculation, not missing from reality — track it down and include it

The number itself isn't the goal — it's a diagnostic. Recalculate it every month using the same method, and the trend line tells you more about your financial direction than any single month's income ever could.
